# Dilla

**A small room that plays a chord progression, soft and a little late, the way a loop sounds when a drummer leaves it alone.** Dilla is one Ruby file and one data file. It reads a progression, plays its chords as stacks of sine waves, swings them, softens them and wraps them in a little reverb, and writes a wav. Add a drum pattern and a kick, a snare and a hat fall on top.

The engine is `dilla.rb`, about two hundred and fifty lines of plain Ruby with no gem beyond the standard library, and the data is `dilla.json`, which sits beside it. Run `ruby dilla.rb --list` to see what the data holds. Run `ruby dilla.rb --progression sv_intro --out out/sv_intro.wav` to render one progression, add `--drums boom_bap` to lay a drum pattern over it, and run `ruby dilla.rb --quick` to render the first progression into `out/quick.wav` as a smoke test. Renders are sixteen-bit mono at 44.1 kilohertz. The folder `out/` is ignored by git, because audio is not tracked, and a render that matters belongs outside the repository, in the dilla-renders folder under your documents.

The data holds ten progressions, three effects presets and three drum patterns. The progressions are named for Slum Village and J Dilla records: sv_intro, sv_fantastic, sv_keep_it_on, sv_look_of_love, sv_estimate, sv_conant_gardens, sv_get_dis_money, sv_fall_in_love, sv_jealousy and dilla_life. Each carries its chord names, a voicing as a list of frequencies in hertz, a tempo, how many beats a chord holds, and the effects preset it plays through. The three presets are dilla_butter, analog_lush and dry; each sets a swing, a detune, an amount of reverb and a low-pass cutoff, and the swing delays every second chord by a fraction of a beat. The three drum patterns are boom_bap, dilla_swing and sparse; each is three rows of sixteen steps, one row for the kick, one for the snare and one for the hat.

Read the attributions with care. The chord names and the records they are credited to were suggested by an outside AI system and have not been checked against a transcription, a score or a recording by anyone here. Treat them as starting voicings to be corrected by ear, not as facts about the records.

The engine is small on purpose, and its limits are plain. The drums play over the first bar only, and then stop, so a long progression is mostly drumless. The chords play through once and the render ends, with no looping and no second pass. Every note is a stack of four sine partials, so the sound is thin and organ-like, not a Moog, and the only motion is the detune and the swing: there is no LFO and no filter that moves. The reverb is four simple delays, and the kick, snare and hat are synthesised, with the noise unseeded, so two renders of the same file differ a little. The old engine, which had a large synthesiser, a drum programmer, samples, a live player and a video renderer, is not lost: the tag dilla-engine-final marks the last commit that carried it, and `git checkout dilla-engine-final -- STUDIO/dilla` brings it back.
