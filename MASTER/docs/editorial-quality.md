# Editorial quality

Editorial quality is assessed separately from engineering quality. A passing
Ruby suite does not establish factual accuracy, fairness, accessibility or
clarity; a well-written document does not establish that its software works.

## Editorial review

- Accuracy: define terms, institutions, populations and time periods precisely.
  Distinguish verified facts from source claims, proposals, inferences and open
  questions.
- Sourcing: attach sources to material factual claims. Retain publication date,
  population, method and limitations. Do not turn an illustrative number into a
  universal statistic.
- Accessibility: use plain language, descriptive headings, meaningful link
  text, readable contrast and a structure that works with assistive technology.
- Clarity: follow Strunk and White's durable advice: prefer concrete words,
  active voice and economical sentences. Omit needless words without removing
  necessary qualifications.
- Balance: describe relevant trade-offs, uncertainty, counter-evidence and
  affected groups. Do not present advocacy as neutral research or false balance
  as fairness.

The SNL quality bar used in this project means accurate definitions, sourced
claims, accessible language, clear explanations and fair representation of
relevant perspectives. It is an editorial rubric, not a formal Law or a claim
of certification.

## Separate verdicts

Report engineering verification and editorial review independently. An
unreviewed editorial dimension is INCONCLUSIVE, not implicitly approved by
passing software tests.
