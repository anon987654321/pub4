## 0. Purpose and rules

This file collapses the FETAT website proposal, related GitHub material, AI³ visualization idea, operational sketches, workforce and finance proposals, and relevant evidence/statistical/legal questions into one maintainable record. Repeated copies are deduplicated; distinct ideas remain represented. Original sources remain the provenance trail.

The website frames FETAT (“FETAT – FAMILIEETATEN”; also written “F-ETAT”) as a citizen proposal to replace Norway's current child-welfare service with a differently organized service for protecting children and families. It is not an existing public agency or enacted policy.

Throughout this document:
- **Source fact** means only that a source says or displays something.
- **Source claim** means a statement from the proposal that has not been independently proved here.
- **Verified external fact** means a statement checked against an identified official source.
- **Proposal** means a possible design choice, not current law.
- **Open question** means that a source, definition, legal authority, or method still needs verification.

Children’s safety, rights, dignity, privacy, participation, development, education and meaningful family relationships must remain central. Preserving a family is not an absolute rule when a child is unsafe; an allegation or official concern is not automatically proof that separation is necessary. A child's views are important and must be heard appropriately, but must not be extracted through leading questions or inferred from facial expression, tone or AI sentiment.

No identifiable case details, names, addresses, case numbers, health information, school information, confidential decisions or recordings belong in this public repository. Individual case reviews require a separate protected workspace, original unmodified records, access controls, a lawful purpose and retention rules.

## 1. Executive summary

The proposal argues that intrusive child-welfare decisions should have clearer evidence, reasons, safeguards, independent review and opportunities to correct error. It proposes a centralized or regional agency with multidisciplinary expertise; accessible reporting and support; rapid initial triage; child-friendly temporary care options; independent review of serious failures; retrospective review of prior care decisions when justified; specialist pathways; and changes to funding and workforce organization.

The consolidated model has three outcomes:

1. **Safety:** protect children from real danger through competent and timely action.
2. **Fairness:** test claims against primary records, consider counterevidence, explain decisions, and allow effective review.
3. **Accountability:** identify and correct mistakes while protecting children, parents, professionals and witnesses from unsupported accusations.

FETAT’s aims should be developed into a costed legal and operational blueprint, rather than implemented verbatim from the source posts. The strongest parts are the call for traceable evidence, meaningful participation, independent scrutiny, early help and placement stability. The most consequential proposals—transferring all welfare cases to criminal courts, mandatory broad family examination, seven-day investigative ceilings, children’s homes as temporary holding arrangements, continuous video surveillance, mass retrospective reviews, ending an entire profession, and large cuts to foster-carer payments—need serious legal, clinical, child-rights, safety and financial analysis before they could responsibly be adopted.

AI³ can help organize documents and show evidence relationships. It must not decide whether a child should be removed or returned, diagnose a person, determine credibility, or convert an allegation into a fact.

## 3. Consolidated institutional model

### 3.1 A state/regional family-protection agency

The proposal seeks to replace the current child-welfare organization with a new regional or national service. It argues that municipalities may face conflicts where the same local ecosystem includes the child-welfare service, school, kindergarten, health provider or other institution implicated in the concern.

A national agency is not automatically independent just because it sits above municipalities. To reduce conflicts, the design needs statutory independence where appropriate, transparent assignment rules, conflict declarations, separation between operational decision-making and appeals, cross-regional review, secure reporting channels, published standards and an external mechanism for investigating the new agency itself.

Questions to resolve:
- Is FETAT one national authority with regional branches or a network of statutory regional units?
- Which duties remain with municipalities, health services, schools, police, prosecutors and courts?
- How is local knowledge preserved while avoiding local conflicts?
- What body can compel corrective action when FETAT itself is at fault?
- How are Sami, minority-language, disability and remote-area needs addressed?

### 3.2 Proposed regional footprint

The website discusses roughly 11–12 regional bases in some places and up to 15 in other variants; its regional map uses 2022 population data and combines some counties/regions. These counts are not a current operational analysis.

Before selecting a footprint, model population, case demand, travel time, after-hours coverage, specialist availability, placement capacity, geography, language needs and workload distribution using current data. Separate physical office location from jurisdiction and from the location of independent reviewers.

### 3.3 Multidisciplinary teams

The proposal names combinations of pediatricians and other physicians, child/adult psychiatrists, psychologists, nurses, specialist child nurses, physiotherapists, dentists, radiology/forensic expertise, police investigators, jurists/lawyers, educators and social-care/disability professionals.

The useful principle is matching expertise to the issue. The unsafe alternative is assuming every family needs every specialist, or that a profession can never be trusted. A professional opinion should be attributable, evidence-based and within scope; it is not automatically a legal finding.

Minimum team design:
- one accountable case lead and a recorded decision-maker;
- written remit for each participating professional;
- conflict declarations;
- recorded disagreement and an escalation route;
- separate clinical, investigative and legal functions;
- evidence-linked reasons for any proposed intrusive action;
- proper participation by the child;
- no default medical or psychiatric examination merely because a report exists.

A unanimity rule may delay urgent protection or encourage superficial consensus. An alternative is an accountable decision by an authorized lead, with dissent and uncertainty recorded and independent review available.

## 4. Intake and investigation time limits

### 4.1 Proposed national hotline “123”

The proposal suggests a national child/family intake number, logging calls, potential recording, digital reporting and possible anonymity. The principle of accessible child-friendly reporting is worth evaluating. The number itself must not be advertised as an operating emergency line unless allocation, routing and responsibility have been confirmed with the competent Norwegian authorities.

Requirements:
- integrate with existing child helplines, police, ambulance, health services and child-welfare intake;
- define emergency versus advice/non-urgent use;
- support children who cannot safely speak or receive callbacks;
- have language and accessibility support;
- protect good-faith reports;
- record a traceable intake ID and triage outcome;
- define lawful recording, notice, access, retention and deletion before recording any call;
- distinguish a maliciously fabricated report from a concern that is simply unsubstantiated;
- test the design with children and safeguarding professionals.

The reporting channel must not discourage genuine concerns by threatening punishment for reports that do not result in a finding.

### 4.2 Proposed 48-hour review and seven-day maximum

The website proposes intensive review for up to 48 hours, extensions in 48-hour increments and a maximum seven-day assessment window in one version, with court involvement if intervention continues.

A deadline can improve promptness, initial safety triage, evidence preservation and communication. It cannot guarantee that every complex matter is resolved accurately in two or seven days. A deadline must not pressure staff to rush interviews, carry out unnecessary examinations or choose a conclusion simply because time is expiring.

Revised workflow:
1. Immediate safety triage and a least-intrusive initial plan.
2. Preserve relevant evidence lawfully and identify what is missing.
3. Conduct an initial assessment of the child's needs, views, risks, protective factors and available support.
4. Record a written first decision with known facts, uncertainty, legal basis and next review date.
5. Before extension, identify unresolved questions, the evidence needed, why delay matters and what safeguards apply.
6. Obtain judicial or other independent authorization wherever the specific legal regime requires it for continued intrusive action.
7. End, refer, support or continue through the correct lawful route, with an exit/review plan.

An investigation deadline is not itself legal authority to restrict a child’s or parent’s liberty.

## 5. Independent control body and retrospective review

### 5.1 Independent control / “havarikommisjon”

The proposal calls for an independent body to review complaints, inspect serious errors, carry out cross-regional checks, examine systemic patterns and refer suspected offences to competent authorities.

A workable design would:
- be separate from the original decision-making chain;
- receive complaints from children, parents and authorized representatives;
- review record completeness, legal procedure, evidence handling and reasons;
- sample closed files and audit recurring problems;
- make recommendations or legally authorized corrective decisions;
- refer potential crimes to police/prosecutors rather than declare criminal guilt itself;
- track implementation and publish anonymized systemic findings;
- preserve the right of affected parties to correct factual errors;
- disclose conflicts and explain the review method.

A body that is independent in name only will not solve institutional conflicts. Conversely, a control body without a clear remit can become a second unaccountable decision-maker. Its powers, procedures, appeal routes, deadlines, access rights and budget must be set in law.

### 5.2 Retrospective review of care decisions

A retrospective review should not presume that all historic decisions were right, nor that they were wrong. It needs a legal mandate, safe access to files, objective selection criteria, reviewers without conflicts, fair notice and responses, and a current assessment of the child's welfare separate from the question of whether the historical decision was justified at the time.

Suggested triage priorities:
- credible new evidence of serious factual error;
- a significant procedural defect;
- newly discovered evidence that could materially affect the outcome;
- ongoing safety risks or serious present harm;
- a pattern suggesting a systemic failure;
- cases selected through a representative audit sample.

Do not order automatic return, prosecution or public condemnation based on an incomplete file or headline. A change in current placement must use the lawful, child-specific process and consider present safety, relationships, wishes and needs.

## 6. Temporary children’s homes, family contact and kinship options

### 6.1 Temporary children’s homes

“Barnehjem – ja takk” proposes regional children's homes as a temporary option in some unresolved cases, with stable adult caregivers, schooling, individualized care, sibling contact and family visits where safe.

A temporary residential unit might be an option for a specific child where justified, but it must not become an automatic holding facility or detention substitute. Institutional care itself can cause harm, so safety cannot be assumed from the name “children’s home.”

Minimum safeguards:
- clear individual legal basis for admission;
- prompt independent review and a defined maximum review interval;
- named caregiver and written care plan;
- education and health continuity;
- safe sibling and parent contact based on the child's needs;
- private and accessible complaint route;
- independent visits, safeguarding inspections and whistleblowing protection;
- a clear exit plan and reasoned extensions;
- lawful, necessary and proportionate handling of any monitoring;
- no presumption that a centre is safer than every foster or family arrangement.

Indiscriminate 24-hour camera surveillance creates serious privacy and dignity risks and cannot replace good staffing, stable relationships, independent visits and a confidential way for children to report harm.

### 6.2 Family and trusted-network placement

The proposal prefers evaluating family/network placement before placement with unknown carers. Continuity, identity and familiar relationships can matter, but kinship alone does not prove suitability.

Assessment should consider the child's own views, safety concerns, caregiver willingness and capacity, stability, family violence or coercion, siblings, support needs and potential loyalty pressure. Record why a family option is chosen or rejected. The principle is “actively assess safe and suitable network options,” not “always place with relatives.”

### 6.3 Contact during review

Family contact should be individualized, safe and reasoned. A blanket rule for unrestricted contact can overlook real risks; a blanket ban can inflict unnecessary harm. Decisions should state the basis, the child's views, relevant safety concerns, review date and what would permit arrangements to change.

## 7. Specialist units proposed by FETAT

### 7.1 Suspected sexual abuse and serious violence

A specialized, coordinated pathway can reduce repeated questioning and delay. It should use trained child-friendly interviewers, trauma-informed procedures, appropriate health care, legally authorized evidence preservation, confidentiality and clear division of responsibility between child protection, medical services, police and prosecutors. It must not prejudge whether an allegation is true or false and must avoid unnecessary repeat interviews.

### 7.2 Parent conflict and contact disputes

The source proposes a specialist path for high-conflict separations and complex contact disputes, with a suggested six-month window for agreement in one version. A fixed deadline must never force unsafe contact or prevent urgent action where there is violence, coercion or abuse. A child's refusal of contact must not automatically be attributed to manipulation, psychiatric illness or proof of an unsafe parent. Distinguish family-law proceedings, child protection and criminal allegations.

### 7.3 School and education settings

The school unit proposal seeks a child-friendly route for bullying, violence, abuse and other harm in school, transport, after-school care and similar settings. The design must map its relationship to existing school duties, education authorities, police, the County Governor (Statsforvalteren) and ombuds services. Allegations against staff require independence from the institution involved, fair process for the accused, proportionate interim safeguards and a clear referral path.

### 7.4 Youth substance use, crime and health needs

Specialist help for young people can be useful, but a young person’s harmful behaviour does not remove their rights. Child protection, criminal responsibility, health treatment and education have different legal bases and should not be collapsed into one undefined investigative power.

## 8. Adjudication and review: civil/child-welfare versus criminal proceedings

The website proposes abolishing the Barneverns- og helsenemnda and transferring child-welfare cases into criminal courts, arguing that criminal proceedings provide a stronger evidence standard. This is a proposal, not current law.

Criminal courts decide criminal responsibility under criminal procedure; child-protection decisions address different legal questions about protection, care and the child's best interests. Transferring all care cases into criminal proceedings could confuse the issues, delay essential protection and put the child in the wrong procedural role.

A more precise reform brief should identify the particular defect to solve: access to the record, disclosure, the ability to challenge evidence, hearing records, reasoned decisions, delay, legal assistance, reviewer independence or an appeal route. Proposals may include stronger evidence disclosure, better records, clear reasons, qualified decision-makers, better legal assistance and effective judicial safeguards without presuming that every welfare case should become a criminal trial.

The website's broad assertions that the existing system has “no evidence requirements” or that a conditional percentage shows the tribunal awards nearly every case to the public party must not be repeated as established legal facts. Check the statute, procedural rules, official report tables and the denominator for each statistic.

## 9. Workforce and funding

### 9.1 Workforce proposal

Some FETAT posts call for phasing out the barnevernspedagog profession and estimate roughly 6,900 staff using historical categories. They propose transitioning some staff into schools, kindergartens, NAV, PPT or other services.

These are a policy position and a historic estimate, not a verified current workforce count or proof that an entire profession is unsuitable. A defensible reform must map roles, qualifications, regional distribution, workload, capabilities and service gaps; define required competencies; plan retraining and recruitment; preserve continuity for children; consult staff and affected families; and evaluate outcomes without making a group-wide presumption of incompetence.

### 9.2 Financial proposal

The finance post quotes 2023 budget figures of NOK 1.9 billion for police and NOK 8.41 billion for child welfare, describes them as Google-derived and assumes they are correct. These are unverified and potentially non-comparable fiscal categories. They should not be used for an official cost case without reconstructing appropriations and expenditure from audited official sources.

A full cost model must include national governance, regional sites, 24/7 intake, case teams, clinical and forensic specialists, family support, temporary residential units, education/health continuity, legal aid, courts, independent oversight, transport, interpreters, training, data security, transition costs, evaluation and contingency.

Reducing foster-carer payments is not a guaranteed saving. Payments can cover costs and responsibilities; reductions can affect placement supply, quality and stability. Separate cost reimbursement from any payment that could create a conflict of interest, then evaluate actual outcomes and availability.

No agency or individual should have a financial incentive to remove a child, retain a child away from family, or avoid a necessary intervention.

## 11. Current legal baseline

**Legal verification rule:** A general statute link does not establish that a particular power, duty or procedure is current or applicable. Before treating a proposition as law, cite the exact section and paragraph in the consolidated text, check amendments and commencement provisions, and identify the date and circumstances to which the rule applies. Distinguish enacted law, provisions not yet commenced, bills/propositions, administrative guidance, case-law interpretation and this document's policy preferences. If the point is unresolved, mark it as an open legal question. A proposed new agency does not automatically acquire existing statutory powers or remove the jurisdiction of an existing body; those changes require explicit legal analysis and, where necessary, legislation.


Norway's principal child-welfare statute is the Barnevernsloven, Act of 18 June 2021 No. 97, in force from 1 January 2023; it replaced the 1992 Act. Historical legal identifiers must not be reused as current law.

## 12. Individual case-review protocol

The original GitHub draft alleges possible problems in a specific family matter: telephone notice, inadequate opportunity to respond, uncertain evidence, insufficient consideration of a child's wishes, inadequate assessment of alleged violence/protective steps, incorrect or incomplete information, missed alternatives and possible family-life rights issues. These are allegations in a draft, not verified facts about a case.

A fair review should proceed as follows:

1. **Define scope:** identify the exact decision, date, authority, jurisdiction, procedural stage, safety question and deadline.
2. **Preserve records:** list original decisions, letters, communications, case notes, meeting minutes, evidence, reports and responses; preserve originals unchanged.
3. **Build a timeline:** separate event date, record creation date, receipt date and decision date. Label estimates and disputed dates.
4. **Classify statements:** distinguish direct observation, a party's account, hearsay, professional opinion, inference, allegation, legal question and established finding.
5. **Create an issue matrix:** precise question, legal rule/time period, supporting evidence, contrary evidence, missing material, reviewer, next action and status.
6. **Check law in force at the time:** use official consolidated text, transitional rules, judgments and competent legal advice.
7. **Test alternatives:** record the strongest supporting evidence, strongest counterevidence, plausible explanations and what would change the preliminary view.
8. **Independent review:** assign appropriately qualified reviewers with conflicts declared and limitations documented.
9. **Choose a lawful route:** depending on the actual decision and dates, this may be access to records, factual correction, complaint, appeal, review or court filing. Confirm the specific route and deadline; this file does not promise a reopening or calculate deadlines.
10. **Close responsibly:** record evidence relied on, dissent, unresolved questions, accountable human decision and retention plan. Any current change of placement must be based on the child's current circumstances and the lawful process.

Do not advise people to evade a lawful process, move simply to avoid a case, or publicize a child's details. Safer general steps are to obtain qualified legal advice promptly, preserve originals, request written reasons and records through the correct channel, correct factual errors in writing, document important interactions lawfully, and challenge decisions through the applicable route.

## 13. AI³ evidence-visualization specification

The GitHub political-platform section proposes a visual evidence map to expose gaps and contradictions and make complex records easier to review. The following constrained system preserves that goal.

### 13.1 Capabilities
- index authorized records and preserve their source identifiers;
- build a date-aware chronology;
- link each claim to a specific source locator;
- map a decision to its stated reasons and evidence;
- flag unreferenced assertions, potential inconsistencies and missing documents;
- distinguish original records, copies, transcripts, summaries, translations and AI-generated transformations;
- show evidence for and against each material claim;
- create an exportable human-review packet;
- preserve corrections, previous versions and reviewer dissent;
- explain what was and was not inspected.

### 13.2 Prohibited uses
- no automated decision on a child's placement or return;
- no determination of a person's credibility from facial, voice or sentiment analysis;
- no mental-health or parental-fitness diagnosis;
- no autonomous interview of a child;
- no claim that repeated copied text is independent corroboration;
- no invented legal citations or facts;
- no suppressed counterevidence;
- no disclosure of confidential data to an unapproved model provider;
- no external communication or public accusation without authorized human review;
- no public child-level evidence maps.

### 13.3 Core views
- **Chronology:** events, communications, assessments, decisions and reviews, with source locators and date uncertainty.
- **Decision/reason map:** each decision, legal basis, stated reasons, evidence, response and review route.
- **Claim/evidence graph:** supporting, contradicting, contextual and inconclusive sources.
- **Issue tracker:** neutral questions, missing material, owner, status and next step.
- **Provenance panel:** original record, custodian, dates, integrity hash, extraction method, transformations and access class.
- **Alternative-explanation panel:** strongest competing interpretations and the evidence that would distinguish them.
- **Child-participation view:** how and when the child was informed and heard, and how the views were considered—not an AI score of sincerity.

### 13.4 Minimum data model

| Entity | Minimum fields |
|---|---|
| Case | Pseudonymous ID, jurisdiction, scope, period, privacy class, accountable owner |
| Evidence item | ID, source/custodian, original location, dates, type, language, integrity hash, access class |
| Source locator | Evidence ID, page/paragraph/timecode/record ID, extraction method |
| Event | Event ID, event/record dates, roles, neutral description, source IDs, time certainty |
| Claim | Neutral wording, claimant/source, date asserted, evidence, status and limits |
| Evidence link | Related IDs, relation type, rationale and reviewer |
| Decision | ID, authority, date, legal basis, outcome, reasons, notice and review route |
| Legal authority | Citation, jurisdiction, version/effective dates, official URL, retrieval date, reviewer |
| Issue | Question, materiality, needed evidence, owner, status and next action |
| Review | Reviewer role/conflicts, scope, findings, limitations, dissent, follow-up |
| Audit/access event | Actor, purpose, time, object, action, reason and export/share event |

Originals remain immutable. Hash records when ingested; record extraction/transcription/translation versions; preserve timestamps and time zones; label unreadable passages; deduplicate copies; and require a source locator for every material factual statement or display “source not located.” The correct result for insufficient evidence is “insufficient evidence,” not a forced answer.

### 13.5 Evaluation
Test with synthetic or lawfully de-identified data. Measure source-citation accuracy, chronology accuracy, unsupported-claim rate, retrieval recall, preservation of counterevidence, hallucinated legal references, transcription/translation errors, reviewer correction rate, accessibility and privacy incidents. Humans own all final conclusions.

FETAT's public advocacy can focus on accountability, timeliness, access to support, meaningful participation, evidence quality, independent review and measurable reform. It should not turn unverified case claims into accusations against identifiable people.

6. No sensitive records in public GitHub.
7. Controlled model use, with a privacy assessment and approved provider configuration.
8. No public demos with identifiable case documents.

10. Independent oversight for any real-world pilot.

## 16. Metrics

Measure outcomes with context. Do not optimize for fewer removals, more removals, fewer complaints or more appeals in isolation.

**Safety and welfare:** urgent-response timeliness; harm while a report is assessed or after intervention; placement stability; access to health and education; child-reported safety where safe to obtain; family-network suitability.

**Procedural fairness:** reasons/notice where required; child's participation; access to records; correction opportunities; review timeliness; reasoned outcomes; conflict declarations.

**Evidence quality:** proportion of factual statements with source locators; unsupported-claim rate; missing records; transcription/translation errors; presence of contrary evidence; AI summary correction rate.

**Efficiency:** case-hours by complexity; specialist wait time; time to first safety plan; travel times; length of placement; cost with defined outcome measures.

**Accountability:** complaint categories and resolution times; substantiated procedural defects; implementation of recommendations; repeat problems; independent audit results.

A lower intervention count does not automatically mean a better system, and a higher count does not automatically mean a worse one. A sound system may intervene decisively in some dangerous cases while preventing avoidable separations in others.

## 17. Critical risk register

| Risk | Required control |
|---|---|
| Allegations presented as facts | Label claim status, cite primary evidence, include response and counterevidence |
| New agency develops new conflicts | Statutory independence, cross-regional review, external control body |

## 20. Compare institutional options before replacing the whole system

The goals of better evidence, independence and accountability are separable from the question of whether every existing institution must be replaced at once.

| Option | Description | Advantage to test | Main risk to resolve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Improve current arrangements | Strengthen records, review, support and audits within current legal structures | Lower transition disruption | May leave structural conflicts unresolved |
| Independent regional investigation | Separate complex investigations from municipal operational interests | More independent fact-finding | Requires precise authority and handoff rules |
| National standards with regional operations | National policy and data standards, regional teams, separate adjudication | Consistency plus local execution | Centralization can itself become opaque |
| Full FETAT replacement | Replace the operational model and redesign oversight, intake and placement | Coherent redesign | Highest legal, staffing, transition and cost risk |
| Targeted pilots | Pilot hotline/triage, evidence audits or independent review first | Tests claims before full-scale change | A pilot may not predict whole-system effects |

Evaluate each against child safety and participation, risk of wrongful separation, speed, legal rights, independent review, privacy, rural access, language accessibility, staffing, cost and reversibility. A credible FETAT blueprint should explain why a full replacement outperforms targeted changes, not assume this simply from the proposal's diagnosis.

## 21. Legal powers matrix required before implementation

For every proposed action, document: purpose; who may request it; who may authorize it; exact legal basis; threshold; consent requirement; urgency exception; maximum duration; notice and disclosure; access to counsel; independent reviewer or court; appeal route and deadline; required record; data access and retention; and remedy for error.

Create separate rows for interviewing a child, obtaining records, clinical examination, home entry, temporary placement, restriction of contact, call recording, room monitoring, extending an intervention, disclosing records to police and sharing data with an AI provider. A general power to “investigate” is not enough to authorize every intrusive action.

Also map the responsible and reviewing institution at each stage. Police, health services, schools, child protection, prosecutors, courts and independent review bodies have different functions. No professional gains legal authority merely by participating in a multidisciplinary team.

## 23. AI³ release tests

For each test fixture, retain the dataset/fixture version, test ID, expected result, actual result, pass/fail status, defect or incident ID, reviewer, execution date, and evidence artifact. A test suite without recorded expected outcomes and actual results is a test plan, not evidence of a passing implementation. Run tests on synthetic or appropriately de-identified material before any live-case pilot.

Add explicit fixtures for: duplicate records and near-duplicates; contradictory dates; missing pages and OCR corruption; unsupported factual assertions; a citation that exists but does not support the claim; invented or broken citations; translation errors; an allegation paired with contrary evidence; a record with no source locator; correction and version history; unauthorized access; unauthorized export; and accidental transmission to an unapproved model provider. Fail the release gate if a material unsupported claim is presented as established, material counterevidence is suppressed, unauthorized disclosure occurs, or source originals cannot be recovered. Passing these tests is evidence about system behaviour under tested conditions, not proof that a legal conclusion or case decision is correct.


A prototype must pass these gates before use with real case material:

1. **Source fidelity:** every material factual claim has a source locator or a visible “source not located” label; duplicate documents are not counted as independent corroboration.
2. **Fair representation:** supporting and contradicting material can both be shown; contested claims remain contested; the system makes no credibility or parental-fitness score.
3. **Safety and privacy:** authorized access is logged; exports are controlled; no public demonstration uses identifiable case records; unapproved external providers cannot receive confidential material.
4. **Human ownership:** reviewers can open original sources, correct and reject summaries, preserve previous versions and approve any final report.
5. **Adversarial evaluation:** test duplicated documents, conflicting dates, missing pages, poor scans, contradictory statements and legal references not present in the source. Measure missed issues as well as false alerts.

The correct outcome when records are insufficient is “insufficient evidence,” not a forced yes/no result. A visually clear map is not proof that its interpretation is correct.

## 24. Public brief — recommended framing

A defensible public brief can say that complex child-welfare decisions require reliable evidence, meaningful child participation, timely support, clear reasons and effective independent scrutiny. It can present FETAT as a proposal to evaluate, with accessible intake, multidisciplinary expertise, source-linked decisions, independent review, family support and published aggregate audits.

The brief should distinguish a personal account, a documented fact, an allegation, a legal interpretation and a proposed reform. Verify numbers against original sources; state units and time periods; remove details that could identify a child; disclose uncertainty; publish corrections; and avoid unsupported claims about all workers or all decisions. The strongest argument is based on evidence and safeguards, not on proving that every person or institution is at fault.
